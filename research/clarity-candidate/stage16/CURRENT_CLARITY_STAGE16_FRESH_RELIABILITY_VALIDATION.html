The answer

The reliability gate removes most of the harmful answers. The fresh holdout is too small to certify it.

  • On development, the gate cuts harmful answers. A state is issued only when its calibrated posterior is at least 70%. That takes harmful answers from 16.6% to 10.6% and severe ones from 2.0% to 1.5%, with the operational score steady at 7/10.
  • The gate never issues a plain "Normal" or "Stained". Neither ever reaches 70% confidence, so the evidence removed them, not a hand-written rule.
  • Tier 2 is now boundary states only. Its harmful rate on development falls from 12% (Stage 15) to 7%. The Stage 15 re-score had shown 29%.
  • Stained water still scores 0.571 on development, below the 0.60 bar. No threshold choice fixes it, because it is the retrieval-resolution limit.

1. The single-state reliability gate (development only)

The posterior model.

  • The fits. Three monotone logistics give P(truth ≥ 6, 3, 2 ft | prediction).
  • The class posteriors follow from their differences.
  • Fitting. Nested in folds for evaluation; on all development lake-held-out predictions for production.

The rule. A single state requires a posterior ≥ τ₁; otherwise the best adjacent pair requires ≥ τ₂; otherwise Unknown.

  • The search. A pre-registered grid of 128 τ₁ × τ₂ × tier-2 combinations.
  • No combination met the primary constraints (harmful ≤ 12%, severe ≤ 1.5%, every class ≥ 0.60), because stained stays below 0.60 everywhere.
  • The fallback rule (the best score with harmful ≤ 15% and severe ≤ 2%) chose τ₁ = τ₂ = 0.70 with tier-2 option T2-D.

10. The shortage, and the sources that would complete it

The size of the gap.

  • The pool. All 2026 WQP data at untouched sites gives 1,111 events, but only 108 matched observations. Of those, 9 are stained and 6 limited, against minima of 60 and 25.
  • What would close it. At this yield (about 10% of events match, and 8% of matches are stained), 60 stained rows need about 7,500 eligible events, roughly 7× today's pool, in murkier water than Wisconsin's.

Independent sources that would close it, all before anyone looks at the values:

  1. The 2026 agency data not yet in WQP. Many states upload a season's data the following spring: FL LAKEWATCH, MN PCA, TCEQ, ADEM, Iowa DNR. A re-pull in about April 2027, under this same frozen protocol, is the cheapest route.
  2. Southern reservoir agencies, by direct request. TVA Reservoir Ecological Health monitoring, and USACE district water-quality programs (Nashville, Mobile, Vicksburg, Tulsa, Fort Worth). This is where stained and limited water lives.
  3. State reservoir programs with their own portals: Oklahoma OWRB BUMP, Missouri LMVP, Texas SWQMIS, Iowa AQuIA, Illinois VLMP.

11. Path from the current score to a true 9/10

The arithmetic. A product whose every answer is a correct boundary state scores 0.90, so 9/10 (≥ 0.85 class-balanced) needs about 90% of every class answered correctly at full authority. Today that is 0.69–0.71. Four things stand in the way:

  1. Resolution. Stained water (2–3 ft) is narrower than the retrieval error (×1.36), so stained answers are mostly boundary states or Unknown (0.57 development). A 9/10 needs more information per answer than one scene can give, such as multi-scene consensus or a later design that assimilates field reports. That means reopening model work with a new pre-registration.
  2. Authority. Lower-authority answers cap at 0.75 and Unknown at 0.5. Sentinel-2C is a third of current scenes (53% of 2026 matches), and it needs to pass its lift criterion: more tandem pairs, and S2C harmonisation re-derived against the low bias now seen on both independent sets.
  3. Evidence. A fresh holdout of ≥ 200 rows with ≥ 60 stained (section 10) is needed to certify any of this.
  4. Strong evidence is 5–10% of answers. Sector Intelligence would need most answers at that level.
