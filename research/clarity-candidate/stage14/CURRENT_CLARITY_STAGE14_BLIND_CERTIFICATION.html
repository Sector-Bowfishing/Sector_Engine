The answer

The frozen engine certifies at the level development predicted. That level is not yet good enough to pass.

  • The engine. C-gbm, Stage 13's best exact-feet model, frozen as a single continuous engine. One prediction gives exact feet, a two-thirds interval (−37% / +51%) and a product state. The boundary bands sit where the odds across 3 ft and 2 ft fall below 2:1.
  • O2 lost the pre-registered comparison: it is worse on decision quality by 0.037, CI excludes 0.
  • The blind test. The sealed WQP tier and the NLA rows give 825 US observations at 675 lakes in 46 states, never used in any fit or selection.
  • Decision quality matched development: decision-balanced accuracy 0.726 against 0.724, and the interval covered 65% of truths against a two-thirds target.

  • The bands come from the engine's own error, not from a label. Near 3 ft, a prediction between 2.63 and 3.80 ft is not twice as likely to be on one side of 3 ft as the other. The same holds near 2 ft between 1.70 and 2.38 ft.

The clear-water bias is a tail problem, concentrated in very deep water. It is neither a constant bias nor a proportional one.

  • Typical clear water is unbiased. 6–8 ft reads +0.04 ft and 8–10 ft reads −0.3 ft.
  • The error grows only above 10 ft: −1.8 ft at 10–13 ft, −3.0 ft at 13–20 ft and −10.7 ft at 20 ft or more. The ≥ 20 ft rows are 14% of clear water but carry 48% of the negative error.
  • Two of the four tests hold: tail problem and concentrated in deep water. The bin medians span 10.8 ft and 0.22 log, so the bias is neither constant nor proportional.
  • The cause is regression to the mean plus saturation, not a miscalibrated engine.
    • Conditioned on its prediction, the engine is calibrated: when it predicts 6 ft or more, the median truth is only +0.2 ft away.
    • Conditioned on the truth, its error falls by 0.37 log per log of true Secchi.
    • Two reasons for that. Any estimator of E[y | p] shrinks extremes. And above about 15–20 ft the water-leaving signal approaches that of pure water, so the reflectance carries little information about how much clearer it is.
  • Product consequence, diagnostic only. "Clear" is right 86% of the time, but an exact figure above about 12 ft is a floor rather than an estimate. Nothing was corrected.

7. The sealed WQP tier

What the sealed tier is. The Stage 1 national WQP Secchi pull: 397,798 records at 20,860 sites, 2018–2026, fetched 2026-09-27.

Why most of it was not usable.

  • Development stages had drawn from it: about 11,800 of its sites appear in a Stage 1–13 sample, draw or readout file.
  • So the protocol, frozen before any of its values were read:
    1. drops every site in a Stage 5–13 file, every lake within 5 km of a Stage 5–13 development station, and every Stage 1–4 record;
    2. drops any site with a 2026 record, so the P2 fresh pool is untouched;
    3. drops Guntersville, and anything within 5 km of the Stage 12/13 blind set.
  • The 5 km rule. It follows the Stage 12 NLA precedent and applies to the corpora that fitted or selected the frozen engine (Stages 5–13). Stage 1–4 analysed only the production champion, so its records are excluded one by one.
  • The strict alternative, which also drops every lake touched in Stage 1–4, would have left 1,901 sites, 87% of them in Minnesota. Rows that would survive that rule too are reported separately as the strict-independence subset.
