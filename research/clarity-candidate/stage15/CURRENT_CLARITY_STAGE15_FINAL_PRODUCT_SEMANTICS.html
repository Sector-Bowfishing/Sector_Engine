The answer

The semantics make the product more useful, but not yet safe enough to freeze.

  • Clear-to-Normal works. On development it raised the operational score from 0.667 to 0.690 and cut harmful answers from 21.8% to 17.3%, so it was adopted. On the re-score its answers are right 90% of the time, and Normal moves from NOT to PARTIAL.
  • The very-clear floor did not trigger. Predictions above 10–15 ft are as precise as any others in log terms. The deep-water error is water being under-read, and a floor on the prediction cannot reach it.
  • Algal water keeps its state. Flagged water is still classified right 77–79% of the time on development, so the frozen rule is a warning modifier, not softening or withholding.
  • Sentinel-2C becomes lower authority. That costs 4% of the certification rows, but 34% of the 2025-dated ones.

The new operational rubric:

  • Score: 7/10 on both development (0.688) and the re-score (0.690).
  • Readiness to freeze is NO, by the frozen rule, for two reasons:
    • harmful wrong answers are 15.6% on the re-score and 16.6% on development, against ≤ 15%;
    • stained water scores 0.598 and 0.563, against ≥ 0.60.

Everything else passes.

  • The frozen trigger was not met. It asked whether exact feet get worse above 10, 12 or 15 ft, measured among rows predicted that deep. They do not: median |log| is 0.13 at every threshold, against the 0.15 trigger, and predictions there are calibrated (truth − prediction −0.6 to +0.2 ft).
  • The −2.3 ft clear bias comes from somewhere else. Very clear water is predicted too low. Of the development rows ≥ 20 ft, 82% are predicted at 12 ft or more (median 15.5 ft), and the rest are predicted lower still.
  • A floor would make the clear exact feet worse, not better. A 12 ft floor would hide the well-predicted high rows and leave the under-read ones published: the clear median moves from −1.75 to −2.21 ft (development).
  • A "12+ ft" display would still be honest, and remains an optional UX choice for Michael. 69% of predictions ≥ 12 ft are truly ≥ 12 ft on development (78% on the re-score), and 94–96% are clear. It is a display choice, not something the accuracy evidence requires.

The detection. It is the existing satellite flag, NDCI > 0.03, on the answering reflectance.

  • On development it catches 70% of in-situ algal water (chlorophyll ≥ 15 µg/L), with 54% precision.
  • On the re-score it catches 84%.

The frozen rule chose "modifier" at both levels. The states issued to flagged water are right 79% (moderate) and 77% (strong) of the time on development. So the product shows:

Why Stage 14's "algae NOT" stands. That verdict measures in-situ algal water against a constant answer: the engine is not better than always guessing "Stained-to-Limited" there. It is not wrong more often than elsewhere. The modifier tells the bowfisher what kind of water it is, without claiming more.

  • The rule. S2C answers keep their state and lose full authority, and their exact feet and interval are withheld. No bias correction was fitted.
  • The evidence. Development S2C rows read only 0.05 log low (n 38), but independent certification rows read 0.19 low (n 23). Neither sample is large enough to calibrate from.
  • When it lifts. At least 60 independent S2C certification rows with |median signed log| ≤ 0.05, and decision quality within 0.05 of S2A/S2B.
  • The cost. Small on this historical set, but S2C is about a third of current-era scenes. Until the rule lifts, a third of today's answers would carry "lower authority".

10. Remaining blockers

  1. Harmful answers next to boundaries (15.6% / 16.6%). Mostly plain "Normal" (3.80–5.00 ft), plain "Stained" (2.38–2.63 ft), and Clear/Normal confusions. Candidate rules (no plain single state inside a boundary zone; tier 2 at lower authority) need pre-registration and fresh data.
  2. Stained water sits at 0.56–0.60 against the 0.60 bar. It is the retrieval-resolution limit found in Stage 13.
  3. No unseen certification data remains. The P2 fresh pool, Michael's Guntersville survey, or a TVA/USACE records request are the only ways to test any further rule.
  4. Sentinel-2C stays lower authority, about a third of current-era scenes, until ≥ 60 independent S2C rows pass.
  5. Michael's ship decision, and the deep-water clear under-read, which is a retrieval limit no display rule fixes.
  6. Bottom-risk still needs depth.
