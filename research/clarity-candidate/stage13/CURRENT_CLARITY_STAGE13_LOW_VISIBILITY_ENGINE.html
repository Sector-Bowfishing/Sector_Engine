The answer

Limited water is largely fixed. Stained water is not, and it looks like a resolution limit rather than a missing signal.

  • The multicomponent models. Red, red-edge, green/red, blue/green and brightness together cut the exact-feet error from ×1.47 (C3) to ×1.36–1.38.
  • Limited water. Recall rises from 0.32 to 0.54–0.65, and every CI excludes 0.
  • Stained water (2–3 ft) stays at 0.21–0.38 recall on every model. The two diagnostics say why:
    • the class is only a factor of 1.5 wide, narrower than the best retrieval's error spread;
    • an unbiased retrieval with that spread would recall stained only 33%;
    • even perfect in-situ turbidity and chlorophyll reach only 48%.
  • Low visibility is mostly particles. In stained and limited water, in-situ turbidity explains 28–30% of the variance (most of what in-situ data explain). Dissolved organic carbon adds 8%, and chlorophyll about 0. Sentinel-2's blue/green colour feature adds nothing there.
  • No pre-registered candidate is a Stage 13 success. All fail the stained bars (recall ≥ 0.45, precision ≥ 0.40).
  • BEST, by the frozen fallback rule, is O2: the linear multicomponent model with class cut-points learned on held-out lakes. Its balanced accuracy is 0.568 against 0.501 (CI excludes 0).
    • It generalises across regions (0.92–0.93 of its lake-held-out stained and limited recall).
    • Its class never flips on identical S2A/S2C 60 m disks once ±12% boundary bands are used.
    • Its cost: normal recall falls from 0.64 to 0.41.
  • Coverage reaches 100% in three labelled tiers:
    • 76% ACOLITE default;
    • 12% relaxed mask (as good as tier 1);
    • 12% Sen2Cor fallback (lower authority; poor in murky water).
  • The blind set is still not ready. Expanding SYKE fills the total and the cool season, but not the US stained, sediment and limited counts.

  • Particles dominate low visibility.
    • In stained and limited water, turbidity explains most of what in-situ data can explain: 0.30 of 0.31 held-out R² (stained and limited rows among the 509 matched), and 0.28 of 0.36 where DOC exists.

  • The satellite sees the particles, not the colour, in murky water. Within stained and limited rows, the particle features carry 0.19 and green/red 0.09. The blue/green colour feature carries 0.00.
  • Stained water is a resolution problem.
    • The class is narrow. 2–3 ft is a factor of 1.5 (0.176 in log10).
    • The retrieval is wider. The best held-out retrieval (C-gbm) has an interquartile error of −0.14 to +0.12.
    • The arithmetic. Blur the true values with that error, and even an unbiased estimator recalls stained only 33%, normal 51%, limited 76% and clear 86%.

  • Limited water was a model-form problem. C3's single red-band form flattens in bright, murky water. The multicomponent features (740/665, green/red, brightness) keep the gradient, and limited recall doubles.

13. What still stands between this and 9/10

Product readiness stays 2/10 (the Stage 10 rule: fresh evidence only).

What is left:

  1. The class definition limits stained recall. 2–3 ft is narrower than any satellite retrieval can resolve, and narrower even than in-situ turbidity and chlorophyll can. There are three honest paths:
    • accept a boundary state ("normal-to-stained") as a supported answer;
    • widen or merge the stained class for Sector Intelligence;
    • require a much more precise retrieval, which nothing tested here provides.
  2. Normal recall (0.41–0.64) trades against stained recall. One cut-point set cannot maximise both.
  3. Tier-3 coverage is weak. The Sen2Cor fallback misclassifies murky water, so 12% of stations need either another ACOLITE recovery path or a class-only low-confidence answer.
  4. Clear water is still under-read by about 2–3 ft in deep lakes. That blocks clear-feet SUPPORTED.
  5. No blind US certification set exists without new data. US stained, sediment, limited and cool-season counts need either a TVA/USACE records request or your approval of a sealed, never-analysed WQP tier.
  6. Fresh evidence (P2's fresh data and the certification) is still the only thing that moves readiness.
  7. Bottom-risk needs a depth layer (the Navionics track).
