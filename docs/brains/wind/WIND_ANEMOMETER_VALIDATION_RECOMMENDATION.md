# Over-water wind truth — what is the minimum useful plan? (recommendation only; nothing purchased)

## Short answer
**Yes, a small amount of over-water wind truth materially improves what Wind can claim — but it does not move the certification score by itself while it sits at 3.1.** Points come from bank-pair and surface truth (dims 6/7, 40 pts); the over-water reference lifts the *cap* (7) and removes the largest unquantified error in every bank answer: how much stronger the wind is over open water than at the nearest airport. **A portable instrument is enough to start; permanent equipment is not needed for the first answers, and hundreds of stations are never needed.**

## Why it matters
All wind validation to date is on land (8 METAR stations); every exposure number assumes the land-referenced wind applies over the water. Standard boundary-layer guidance (not verified here) says over-water speed exceeds nearby land speed, more so with long fetch. If the ratio is 1.15 the whole HIGH FETCH threshold table shifts one class at 12 mph; if it is 1.0 it does not. One number moves a lot of the product.

## Minimum useful plan (planning estimates; the numeric targets are mine, not canonical)
| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What to measure | 10-minute (60-s sampled) mean speed, direction FROM, peak gust; sensor height above water recorded; water-surface class and a photo; at the same time the nearest land station value is pulled automatically from METAR |
| How many | **≥ 30 sessions** (each one the mean of ≥ 10 min on open water at mid-fetch), of which **≥ 10 with land wind ≥ 8 mph**, **≥ 3 wind-direction quadrants**, **≥ 8 evening/night sessions**. If the session-to-session spread of the over-water/land ratio is about 20% (an assumption), 30 sessions pin the mean ratio to roughly ±4%, enough to see a 10–15% bias. Clustered weather days reduce the effective count, hence the quadrant and night requirements. |
| How many lakes | **2 minimum, ideally 3**: one Tennessee River lake (Guntersville, long fetch), one non-Tennessee lake (Hartwell or a plains/natural lake). The Tennessee reaches are one environment. |
| Conditions that matter most | Steady wind 8–15 mph aligned with the longest fetch; flagged "could turn breezy" nights (also tests the risk flag); evenings. Calm sessions add nothing. |
| Accuracy needed | Speed ±0.3 m/s or 3%, whichever is larger; direction ±10°; averaging and height documented; instrument verified side-by-side with a second device (or a nearby ASOS) before the season; conversion to 10 m with a stated profile assumption. |
| Portable vs permanent | **Portable (handheld on a pole, or boat-mounted at ≥ 1.5 m with a documented height) is enough for the ratio and for pair sessions, needs no permit, and trusted testers can do it.** It cannot give a continuous record, tonight's evolution, or the nowcast gain (−16% at +1 h) shown by an on-lake sensor. |
| Permanent station | Optional second step: a fixed research-grade anemometer on an existing dock at Guntersville (mast 4–5 m above full pool, 1 Hz sampling with 3-s running-max gust and 2/10-min vector means; hardware cost by quote, detail in the private planning notes). A fixture over TVA water needs a federal shoreline permit (Section 26a, months of lead time) and a dock host; host questions are still unasked. |
| Does it earn certification points? | Not directly at 3.1. A qualifying over-water record removes the cap 7; points still need dims 6/7. Whether a *portable* record qualifies under Evidence Addendum v1 (it asks for a documented A-class direct measurement) is a ruling for the Addendum's owner — design the portable protocol to meet it (calibration, height, averaging, time sync, session count) and ask before relying on it. |
| Can trusted testers collect it? | Yes with the handheld protocol; it is the same visit as a bank-pair session. |
| Estimated validation value | High: it converts the largest unknown (over-water ratio) into a measured number with ≈ 30 combined sessions, riding on the pair programme at near-zero marginal effort. |

## Recommendation
1. **Start portable now** (no purchase decision needed beyond a calibrated handheld/boat-mounted unit — Michael's call): add the wind reading to every bank-pair session at mid-fetch.
2. **Decide on one fixed station only after** the portable ratio is known and the Guntersville host/26a questions are answered.
3. Do not buy sensors for other lakes; validation across lakes should come from portable sessions and testers.
