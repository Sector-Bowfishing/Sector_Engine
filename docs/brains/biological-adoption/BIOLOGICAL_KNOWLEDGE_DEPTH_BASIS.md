## Product implications
* **B matches what Intelligence can use.** Intelligence rates only species with Lake-Brain presence; the other 15 rows (redhorses, shad, drum, catfish, paddlefish, mullet, snakehead, goldfish, bowfin, tilapia …) never reach a lake evaluation today. Under A, Biological's score is held down by species the product does not assess.
* **A matches the registry Biological publishes** and cannot be accused of a convenient subset. It rewards research on species that have no customer or Intelligence path.
* Under A the cheapest way to raise the number is to research obscure fish (low bowfishing value); under B it requires better knowledge of the fish Sector actually reports (high value). B points effort in the right direction; A points it away.

## Fairness / consistency
* The other Brains score against their **supported scope** (Lake Brain: profiled lakes; Wind: supported lakes), not against everything that could exist. B is the consistent analogue.
* A rubric change after seeing a favourable number is exactly what must not happen quietly. **Preconditions for B:** publish it as `biological-state-score-v2`, **re-base the history** (the 3.5 → 4.0 move would be a definition change), compute `assessedCoverage` on the same 12-species basis (otherwise the dimensions disagree on what "the species" are), and state the change on the score card.
* B has a blind spot: a median over 12 can hide a very weak species (black buffalo 1.0, black carp 1.0). Recommend publishing the minimum alongside, not folding it into the median.

## Recommendation
Adopt **B**, as a versioned rubric change decided **before** the next score is read, with `assessedCoverage` re-based and the history restated. **Leave the decision to Michael.** Until then the canonical scorer (reading A) stays as is and every report in this campaign states the basis explicitly.
