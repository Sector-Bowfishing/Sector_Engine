# Freshwater fish movement sweep: Bowfisher Knowledge Score (READ-ONLY DIAGNOSTIC)

Practical movement knowledge for every freshwater-primary fish in Biological (plus five registry splits: river carpsucker, quillback, highfin carpsucker, blue tilapia, spotted sucker), gathered from agency/federal/extension/reference sources and existing packets. **This is not field validation, regional validation, human review, or production readiness, and it changes nothing**: the canonical Biological score (4.6), the rubric, the rules, labels and policy are untouched. Full cards, sources and rule candidates stay in the private research store; this file is the digest.

Bowfisher Knowledge Score (0-10) = weights seasonal 15, spawn 20, temperature 15, depth 15, hydrology 10, vegetation/habitat 10, night+light 10, current 5. Each component is the researcher's anchored self-score of completeness and usefulness, capped by how much of the card is actually filled; never citation count. Whole score capped at 3.0 with no credible non-anecdotal source. `generalConfidence` (does the behaviour exist) is kept separate from `regionalConfidence` (does it calibrate to the Southeast).

## Product language: bowfisher takeaways
| Fish | Bowfisher takeaway | General / regional confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Common carp | A bowfisher looking for common carp should concentrate on shallow, muddy, vegetated or newly flooded coves, backwaters and creek mouths when water reaches about 60-72 F in spring (especially on a rising or just-stable stage after rain), then on dusk-to-early-morning shallow flats through summer, and should treat cold winter water and strongly falling stages as low-odds. | HIGH/MEDIUM |
| Alligator gar | A bowfisher looking for this fish (where legal; heavily regulated and vulnerable) should concentrate on freshly flooded vegetated flats, backflooded floodplain tributaries and back-of-cove vegetation during spring rises at 20 C or warmer, and treat cold, falling or stable-low water as deep, bunched, non-shallow conditions. | MEDIUM/MEDIUM |
| Grass carp | A bowfisher looking for grass carp should concentrate on shallow submersed-vegetation beds (hydrilla, naiad, Chara) in coves, shoals and creek arms, and on tributary mouths during spring rises when water is 59-75 F; at night and early morning fish range more, and mid-day warm periods can find them resting in backwaters; if the vegetation is gone expect few, thin fish along algae or marginal plant margins. | MEDIUM/MEDIUM |
| Spotted gar | A bowfisher looking for spotted gar should concentrate on clear, quiet, vegetated backwaters, weedy coves and brushy shoreline within ~10 m of the bank, especially April-June at about 68-86 F and at night, and on warm calm low-water days when fish hang at the surface. Warm rising water pushes them onto flooded margins; falling water packs them into oxbows. Spotted and shortnose gar cannot be told apart at night. | MEDIUM/MEDIUM |
| Northern snakehead | A bowfisher looking for northern snakehead should concentrate on very shallow (under 2 ft to 4 ft) vegetated slow margins, hydrilla and milfoil edges, docks and ditches in late April through September, mostly by day, especially a few days after heavy rain; check local regulations first. | MEDIUM/LOW |
| Silver carp | A bowfisher looking for silver carp should concentrate on tributary mouths, backwater entrances, wing-dike or channel-border slack water and tailwater schools when the river is rising and water is above ~62-64 F, and on warm coves and creek arms in cold weather; expect fish to launch after the boat passes and to the sides and rear, so approach slowly and shoot fish that are not yet spooked, especially at night along shorelines in <=5 m. | MEDIUM/LOW |
| American shad | A bowfisher looking for American shad should concentrate on tailraces and gravel riffles of Atlantic-slope rivers in April-June when water is about 50-70 F, in the evening and night, but harvest is moratorium, catch-and-release or hook-and-line only in many states, and the species does not occur in Southeast reservoirs; legality is the main blocker. | MEDIUM/LOW |
| Bigmouth buffalo | A bowfisher looking for bigmouth buffalo should concentrate on flooded vegetation, grass flats and warm backwater or upper-embayment shallows in spring when water is rising and 58-65F, and on warm, silty, still backwaters in summer; in non-flood years expect fewer shallow runs. | MEDIUM/LOW |
| Bowfin | A bowfisher looking for bowfin should concentrate on shallow, weedy, still backwaters, sloughs and coves at night, especially spring at 58-66 F when males sit on nests near stumps and flooded grass, and watch for surface air gulps in warm low-oxygen weed beds. | MEDIUM/LOW |
| Longnose gar | A bowfisher looking for longnose gar should concentrate in spring (about 64-70 F, April-June) on tailwaters, rocky riffles, gravel shoals and shallow weedy flats where fish stack up, and in summer on shoreline cover and sparse vegetation along river channels and reservoir edges. Winter fish sit deep and slow. Night behaviour is unproven. | MEDIUM/LOW |
| Gizzard shad | A bowfisher looking for gizzard shad should concentrate on protected coves, bays and riprap shorelines at dusk and night during spring spawn (water about 60-70 F) and on YOY schools near shore in summer. | MEDIUM/MEDIUM |
| Goldfish | A bowfisher looking for goldfish should concentrate on shallow vegetated coves, backwater and wetland entrances and warm marshy margins from mid-spring through summer when water is above ~55-60 F, and ignore deep un-vegetated water in fall and winter; they are small (about 12-14 in) so check that the regulations allow taking them. | MEDIUM/LOW |
| Freshwater drum | A bowfisher looking for freshwater drum should concentrate on silty flats, woody cover and tailwater edges at night, and on river and tributary mouths during the spring run (mid-60s F and warming), since drum are mobile benthic fish with no shallow nest event and spots shift with flow. | MEDIUM/MEDIUM |
| Paddlefish | Movement knowledge only (protected species, not a target): paddlefish winter in deep sheltered water and run to flowing gravel reaches in April-May when rising flows meet 50-65 F water, so riverine reaches and tailwaters hold them then. | MEDIUM/MEDIUM |
| River carpsucker | A bowfisher looking for river carpsucker should concentrate on silty backwaters, slow pools, tributary mouths and sand/silt reservoir flats with woody cover, in late spring through midsummer at about 65-80 F, especially at night during rising or flowing tributary conditions, and expect fish to bolt to deeper water on noise in muddy shallows. | MEDIUM/LOW |
| Quillback | A bowfisher looking for quillback should concentrate on tributary riffles and gravel run approaches during April-early June runoff pulses at roughly 45-70 F, plus tailwater pools below dams, and expect fish in quiet moderately clear pools otherwise, with hot calm afternoons and early evening best for inshore fish. | MEDIUM/LOW |
| Smallmouth buffalo | A bowfisher looking for smallmouth buffalo should concentrate on creek mouths, backwaters, flooded shallows and tailrace slack water during spring rises with water in the upper 50s-60s F, and on nearshore flats and backwater shorelines at night in summer, expecting them deeper by day and in fall/winter. | MEDIUM/MEDIUM |
| Shorthead redhorse | A bowfisher should concentrate on gravel riffles and shoals, tributary mouths and tailwater runs in April-May when water is roughly 10-16 C; outside the run these fish sit in 2-7 m runs and are rarely shootable. | MEDIUM/LOW |
| Channel catfish | A bowfisher (where take is legal) looking for channel catfish should concentrate on rocky and wood-cover banks, rip-rap, log jams and tributary mouths in rivers, working after dark when the fish move and feed, mostly late spring through fall; in spawn season males are tucked in cavities and are not exposed. Sight-shooting feasibility is a visibility and legality question, not answered by the biology. | MEDIUM/LOW |
| Tilapia | A bowfisher looking for blue tilapia should concentrate on shallow sandy or weedy flats and canal margins with visible nest pits when water is above about 68 F, and on warm springs, outfalls and effluent channels in winter. Fish are only present in warm southern or heated systems (FL, TX, southern power-plant reservoirs; not established in TVA cold-water reservoirs). Night behavior is unknown. | MEDIUM/MEDIUM |
| Blue sucker | Not a realistic bow target: a swift, deep, rocky large-river fish that is state-protected or of concern in much of its range (TX threatened, ND/MT concern per snippets); only spring tributary-mouth/riffle runs put it in shallow range, and legality should be checked before any shot. | MEDIUM/LOW |
| Bighead carp | A bowfisher looking for bighead carp should concentrate on slow deeper water behind structure, backwater entrances and tributary mouths at dusk and early night when the river is rising or warm; do not count on them to jump, and expect them to be rare in TVA waters except the lower Kentucky Lake and tailwaters. | LOW/LOW |
| Spotted sucker | A bowfisher in the Southeast should concentrate on connected backwater sloughs and clear pools plus spring (Mar-May) gravel riffles in tributaries at about 12-19 C; it is a small, nongame sucker with no known night behavior, so expect to find it incidentally. | MEDIUM/MEDIUM |
| Silver redhorse | A bowfisher should concentrate on tributary gravel shoals and creek mouths feeding Tennessee Valley-type reservoirs from late February to April when water is near 9-15 C; this is the earliest redhorse run and shallow availability outside it is unknown. | LOW/MEDIUM |
| Golden redhorse | A bowfisher should look at gravel riffles and run mouths of tributary streams in April to early June when water reaches the mid-teens to low-20s C, possibly at night; outside that window position in shootable depth is unknown. | LOW/LOW |
| Shortnose gar | A bowfisher looking for this fish should concentrate on connected, slow, turbid backwaters, oxbows and sloughs off big rivers, especially flooded grassy shallows in spring (about 16-24 C) and surface-rolling fish in slack water in warm months; treat identification versus spotted gar and legal status as a separate check. | LOW/LOW |
| Highfin carpsucker | A bowfisher looking for highfin carpsucker should concentrate on clear, gravel-bottom medium-to-large rivers and dam tailwaters (rare in muddy waters and many northern/western states, sensitive species in Kansas) in late spring to early summer at about 55-77 F, in shallows and overflow areas of streams; check local regulations and identification versus river carpsucker. | MEDIUM/LOW |
| Black carp | A bowfisher should treat black carp as a rare, bottom-oriented fish of big-river outside bends, side channels and oxbows over soft bottom, most capturable in warm mid-summer on falling water; there is no documented surface, shallow or night behaviour, so do not plan a trip around them and learn the identification and the legal rules. | LOW/LOW |
| Black buffalo | A bowfisher looking for black buffalo should concentrate on tailwaters and swift deep river reaches most of the year, and on flooded margins, sloughs and tributary mouths in April-May when water is rising and in the mid-60s F, treating identification with caution. | LOW/LOW |
| River redhorse | River redhorse is a clean, fast-gravel-river mollusc feeder that is threatened in places (Michigan, Canada); a bowfisher should treat it as a spring-run fish on gravel shoals in late spring and avoid shooting where the species is protected or ID is uncertain. | LOW/LOW |
